The problem

Learning gaps already exist. Disruption makes them worse.

Many learners in vulnerable communities struggle with foundational learning and unequal access to support. When classroom attendance is interrupted by insecurity, displacement or other disruption, the same learners risk falling even further behind.

Central challenge How can schools support learning every day and keep it going when normal classroom access is disrupted?
01

Weak foundational support

Learners who need extra literacy or numeracy help often receive limited targeted support.

02

Digital access is unequal

Continuous internet, devices and electricity cannot be assumed in every vulnerable community.

03

Disruption breaks routines

When physical attendance is interrupted, schools need a simple way to keep materials, teachers and families connected.

Our solution

One system. Five connected pillars.

EduBridge is useful during normal school days and becomes a continuity system when disruption occurs.

Pillar 01

Foundational Learning

Teachers use simple learner checks and short support sessions to strengthen core literacy and numeracy before gaps become bigger.

Continuity mode

Continue learning during short or long disruption

Existing learner data is only the starting point. EduBridge keeps learning moving through repeated curriculum-aligned cycles.

Education continuity
EduBridge supports learning continuity only. It does not replace emergency services, security agencies or official instructions.

Set the disruption plan

How long disruption is handled

Not one old pack repeated for months.

The latest learner record gives the baseline. Every new cycle introduces or reinforces curriculum-aligned learning, followed by a short check that determines what comes next.

01BaselineLatest learner record + short starting check.
02TeachNew 1–2 week learning pack aligned to level and curriculum.
03CheckShort reassessment or teacher/parent follow-up.
04AdaptAdvance, reinforce or simplify the next cycle.
05Re-enterReturn-to-school assessment and catch-up plan.

Example: if disruption lasts 5 months

Month 1Stabilise

Baseline, priority support and first learning cycles.

Month 2Progress

New content + short reassessments.

Month 3Continue

Move through the curriculum sequence at the learner's demonstrated level.

Month 4Recover

Extra support for learners falling behind or missing learning cycles.

Month 5Prepare return

Final continuity check and re-entry priorities.

Offline-first by design

Internet helps. It is not the foundation.

The core learning experience can still work when connectivity is weak or unavailable.

▤

Printable packs

Core materials can be printed before they are needed.

↓

Download once

Resources can be saved when connectivity is available and reused later.

⌂

Home support

Parents receive simple guidance rather than complex digital instructions.

✓

Simple tracking

Paper and digital follow-up can work together instead of depending on one platform.

Pilot plan

Small enough to test. Structured enough to learn.

A six-month pilot can test whether the model improves everyday support and disruption readiness before scale-up.

Month 1Baseline & setup

School profile, learner needs, teacher orientation and materials planning.

Months 2–3Everyday learning

Foundational clinics, teacher support and learning pack use.

Months 3–4Community follow-up

Parent guidance and simple learner participation tracking.

Month 5Continuity simulation

Test the offline workflow without waiting for a real disruption.

Month 6Evaluate & improve

Review learning outcomes, reach, usability and readiness gaps.

Stronger learning. Stronger school resilience.
